# Niche Comparison: Preliminary Evidence

Research started August 2026. Search-result observations are discovery evidence, not proof of repeatable channel economics.

## Initial signal

| Criterion | Astronomy/cosmology | City history through food and architecture |
|---|---|---|
| Demonstrated audience | Very strong | Strong in adjacent formats |
| Direct competition | Very high | Lower for the exact intersection |
| New-channel evidence | Not established yet | Not established yet |
| Visual-style fit | Excellent | Excellent |
| Accuracy burden | Very high; current science and technical nuance | High; historical disputes and cultural nuance |
| Copyright exposure | Potentially low with original animation and properly sourced public assets | Moderate due to photos, archival material, maps, art, and travel footage |
| Founder interest | High | High but less clearly stated |

## Astronomy/cosmology observations

YouTube search prominently returned established institutions and channels: CrashCourse, Kurzgesagt, PBS/NOVA, Stanford, Perimeter Institute, Professor Dave Explains, History of the Universe, Astrum, SEA, and AstroKobi. Several videos have millions of views, confirming demand. Examples observed include AstroKobi's 3.8M-view physics video, History of the Universe's 17.6M-view long documentary, and a 5.7M-view space video from Good Enough.

This also reveals the challenge: search discovery is dominated by trusted incumbents, institutions, polished animation, or sensational packaging. We still need evidence that newer/smaller channels can earn repeatable long-form views.

### Small-channel case: SciMind

SciMind showed approximately 23.6K subscribers and 43 videos. Its latest 10 observed long-form uploads ranged from 667 to 9,198 views, with a median of roughly 2,119. Most were 19–30 minutes; topics used high-curiosity packaging such as terrifying cosmic events, strange signals, impossible objects, and simulation theory.

This is evidence that a newer science channel can reach monetizable scale, but not yet strong evidence of attractive economics. Median recent views were under 10% of subscribers, and expensive 20–30 minute production could be difficult to recover through ads. The result is **mixed/weak**, not a success proof.

## City/food/architecture observations

The exact search intersection produced mixed evidence. A direct recent example—“Belgium Travel Documentary: History, Food & Architecture”—showed only 110 views after two months. This is a warning that combining attractive subjects does not automatically create demand.

Adjacent formats show substantial demand: Academy Travel's Turin video showed about 265K views in ten months; TDC's Chicago documentary about 364K in one year; Manuel Bravo's “Rome Explained” about 3.1M in three years; CNA Insider's Singapore hawker food-and-architecture documentary about 282K over five years.

The opportunity may be differentiated, but its exact packaging is less validated than astronomy. Strong adjacent videos cannot prove that our combined concept will work.

## Current verdict

- **Astronomy wins on proven broad demand and natural fit with geometric animation.**
- **City/food/architecture wins on differentiation but currently has weaker direct-format evidence.**
- Neither concept passes the business gate yet because small/new-channel repeatability remains unproven.
