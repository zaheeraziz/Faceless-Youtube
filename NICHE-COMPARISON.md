# Niche Comparison: Decision-Ready Research

Research started August 2026. Search-result observations are discovery evidence, not proof of repeatable channel economics.

## Executive conclusion

**Advance astronomy/cosmology as the leading passion-business hypothesis, but do not commit the channel yet.** It has clearer direct audience demand, an excellent fit with geometric/3D animation, a global evergreen library, and lower dependence on third-party footage. However, the SciMind case shows that reaching 20K+ subscribers does not guarantee enough recurring views to support expensive production.

Keep city history through food and architecture as the comparison concept. It has stronger differentiation, but the exact combined format remains less validated than its separate adjacent categories.

The next authorized activity is **packaging and economics validation**, not buying tools or building the channel.

## Astronomy/cosmology observations

YouTube search prominently returned established institutions and channels: CrashCourse, Kurzgesagt, PBS/NOVA, Stanford, Perimeter Institute, Professor Dave Explains, History of the Universe, Astrum, SEA, and AstroKobi. Several videos have millions of views, confirming demand. Examples observed include AstroKobi's 3.8M-view physics video, History of the Universe's 17.6M-view long documentary, and a 5.7M-view space video from Good Enough.

This also reveals the challenge: search discovery is dominated by trusted incumbents, institutions, polished animation, or sensational packaging. We still need evidence that newer/smaller channels can earn repeatable long-form views.

Other observed signals:

- Actual Space showed recent long documentaries with approximately 506K and 719K views, suggesting continuing demand for long-form universe documentaries.
- Boring Space showed approximately 170K views on a seven-month-old two-hour video, but sleep content serves a different viewer job than our active-watching goal.
- Cosmic Revelations showed only 631 views after one year on a 48-minute cosmology explainer, demonstrating that subject demand alone does not create discovery.
- Space, But Messier! showed about 14K views after three years on a short introductory astronomy video—modest evidence, not a breakout business case.
- Cosmo Explains was too new to assess. Early views cannot support a conclusion.

Channels built around sleep, copied documentary footage, religious cosmology, institutions, or celebrity scientists are excluded as direct business comparables even when their videos prove topic demand.

### Small-channel case: SciMind

SciMind showed approximately 23.6K subscribers and 43 videos. Its latest 10 observed long-form uploads ranged from 667 to 9,198 views, with a median of roughly 2,119. Most were 19–30 minutes; topics used high-curiosity packaging such as terrifying cosmic events, strange signals, impossible objects, and simulation theory.

This is evidence that a newer science channel can reach monetizable scale, but not yet strong evidence of attractive economics. Median recent views were under 10% of subscribers, and expensive 20–30 minute production could be difficult to recover through ads. The result is **mixed/weak**, not a success proof.

## City/food/architecture observations

The exact search intersection produced mixed evidence. A direct recent example—“Belgium Travel Documentary: History, Food & Architecture”—showed only 110 views after two months. This is a warning that combining attractive subjects does not automatically create demand.

Adjacent formats show substantial demand: Academy Travel's Turin video showed about 265K views in ten months; TDC's Chicago documentary about 364K in one year; Manuel Bravo's “Rome Explained” about 3.1M in three years; CNA Insider's Singapore hawker food-and-architecture documentary about 282K over five years.

The opportunity may be differentiated, but its exact packaging is less validated than astronomy. Strong adjacent videos cannot prove that our combined concept will work.

Other observed signals:

- A Budapest architecture/art/food vlog showed only 748 views after nine months; host-led travel vlogs are not direct faceless comparables.
- Academy Travel's Turin documentary showed about 265K views in ten months.
- TDC's Chicago history documentary showed about 364K views in one year.
- Manuel Bravo's “Rome Explained” showed about 3.1M views in three years, validating architecture-led city explanation at high quality.
- CNA Insider's Singapore hawker culture documentary showed about 282K views over five years, validating food/architecture history as an episode theme.
- Existing food-history evidence from OTR and Tasting History proves adjacent demand but relies heavily on host personality, travel, or cooking.

## Current verdict

- **Astronomy wins on proven broad demand and natural fit with geometric animation.**
- **City/food/architecture wins on differentiation but currently has weaker direct-format evidence.**
- Astronomy becomes the leading hypothesis because passion lowers long-term founder fatigue and the visual concept naturally supports difficult explanations.
- Neither concept passes the production gate because attractive unit economics and repeatable small-channel discovery remain unproven.

## Business risks by concept

### Astronomy

- Credibility gap: the owner is learning, not presenting as an expert.
- Expensive expectations: viewers compare against elite animation and authoritative hosts.
- Sensational titles can attract clicks while destroying trust.
- Science changes; scripts need current authoritative review.
- AI-generated cosmic imagery can blur observation, simulation, and artistic interpretation.

### City/food/architecture

- The promise may be too broad unless every episode has one central historical question.
- Authentic footage, photography, maps, art, and music create licensing costs.
- Cultural simplification and disputed food-origin claims create credibility risk.
- Host-led competitors have personality and travel access that faceless production must replace with exceptional storytelling.

## Decision gates

Before either pilot is authorized:

1. Create 10 title/thumbnail packages for each concept.
2. Score every package for curiosity, clarity, source depth, visual feasibility, and copyright risk.
3. Estimate realistic cost and hours for one 6–8 minute episode in each concept.
4. Require the astronomy pilot to explain a difficult idea clearly without fake certainty or generic space footage.
5. Require the city pilot to connect city, food, and architecture through one causal historical story.
6. Set a maximum combined validation budget before subscribing to tools.

## Recommended decision

Do not merge astronomy with the city concept. They serve different audience expectations and would weaken channel identity.

Test them as two separate product hypotheses. Astronomy gets the first packaging test because it has the higher score and stronger founder passion. City/food/architecture remains the challenger. Only the stronger package and economics case earns a pilot.

## Evidence limitations

- YouTube does not expose channel RPM or production cost publicly.
- Search results are personalized and change over time.
- Subscriber counts and visible views do not prove profit.
- Only SciMind received a latest-10 channel-level review in this round; other observations are video-level signals.
- The available direct small-channel sample is insufficient for a confident financial forecast.

Therefore, this research supports a **controlled packaging test**, not a claim of product-market fit.
