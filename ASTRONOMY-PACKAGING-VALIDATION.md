# Astronomy Packaging Validation

## Method

Concept scores are hypotheses, not audience-test results. Each factor is scored 1–5:

- **Appeal — 30%:** clear curiosity and stakes for a general adult audience.
- **Accuracy — 20%:** can the promise be supported without misleading simplification?
- **Opportunity — 15%:** differentiation from common space-channel packaging.
- **Visuals — 20%:** supports geometric construction, animation, maps, or 3D reveals.
- **Feasibility — 15%:** can an 8–12 minute pilot be produced without excessive cost or licensing?

## Recommended first package

### Before GPS, the Sky Was the Map

Why it leads:

- Immediately understandable modern contrast.
- Combines astronomy, navigation, history, and practical stakes.
- Naturally supports circles, angles, star paths, maps, and 2D-to-3D animation.
- Can be produced mainly with original diagrams rather than copyrighted footage.
- Broad enough to attract non-space enthusiasts, but focused enough for one clear payoff.

### Scope guardrail

Do not promise the complete history of celestial navigation. The pilot should answer:

> **How could a traveler use the sky to estimate where they were—and what could the sky not tell them?**

This enables a clean story around direction, latitude, Polaris, local limitations, southern-sky methods, and why longitude remained harder. Cultural examples must be carefully scoped and sourced rather than compressed into a superficial global montage.

### Title variants to test

1. **Before GPS, the Sky Was the Map**
2. **How Sailors Found Their Way Using Only the Stars**
3. **How One Star Could Tell You Where You Were**

### Thumbnail variants to test

1. **Fixed Star:** ship below, Polaris above, rotating circular trails, no text.
2. **Lost vs Found:** dark ocean split with chaotic stars on one side and geometric navigation lines on the other.
3. **NO GPS:** satellite icon crossed out; star and latitude arc guide the ship. Maximum two words.

## High-risk packages

- **James Webb found something that shouldn't exist:** strong click potential but overused and easily misleading; excluded from the top ten recommendation.
- **AI discovers alien life:** acceptable only if the episode emphasizes detection methods, false positives, and uncertainty—not imaginary aliens.
- **Civilization-specific invention claims:** high controversy and attribution risk. Frame around documented methods, scholars, instruments, and knowledge exchange.

## Audience test required

Internal scores do not validate demand. Before production:

1. Produce rough, low-cost thumbnail mockups for the top three titles only.
2. Ask at least 20 target viewers which video they would click and what they expect to learn.
3. Reject responses from friends who are merely being supportive; record the reason for each choice.
4. A package passes only if at least 40% choose it against the other two and their expected content matches the intended promise.
5. If no package passes, revise positioning before building a video.

## Business verdict

The “humanity reading the sky” position produces stronger differentiation than generic cosmology while preserving modern hooks. The leading pilot package is commercially testable, visually distinctive, and relatively rights-safe. It has **not** demonstrated product-market fit until external viewers respond.
