# Visual Style Direction

## Core signature

Create complex images from simple geometric shapes in slow, deliberate motion:

- Place a square over a larger square and continue layering.
- Repeat the construction language with rectangles, triangles, and circles.
- Use scale, rotation, alignment, masking, depth, and color to transform basic geometry into recognizable objects.
- Let the flat 2D composition gradually gain depth and transition into a 3D scene.
- Use smooth camera movement to reveal how the finished object was constructed.

## Example sequence

1. A small circle appears.
2. Larger circles and rectangles assemble around it.
3. The composition becomes a plate, bowl, building, map, or landmark.
4. Shadows and perspective introduce depth.
5. The camera rotates and the illustration becomes 3D.
6. The completed object transitions into the next story scene.

## Application to the channel

- Build a city's skyline from rectangles and triangles.
- Construct a local dish from circles, curves, and layered textures.
- Transform map shapes into roads, trade routes, ingredients, or architecture.
- Use geometric assembly to explain historical change over time.
- Reuse a small visual vocabulary so viewers recognize the channel immediately.

## Architecture as the visual bridge

Use repeated symmetry and geometry to connect city history, architecture, and food:

- Reveal how squares, circles, triangles, arches, grids, and tessellations form famous buildings and sculptures.
- Use landmarks such as European churches, the Alhambra in Al-Andalus, and the Taj Mahal as potential case studies.
- Animate a structure from its smallest motif outward: tile, arch, façade, building, then city.
- Transition matching shapes between architecture and food—for example, a dome into a bowl, a rose window into a plate, or a tile pattern into arranged ingredients.
- Explain what the geometry reveals about religion, trade, technology, power, climate, and cultural exchange.

The architecture must be historically accurate. Do not assume that visual symmetry has the same meaning across cultures or periods.

## Production approach

- Remotion can handle 2D shape animation, timing, masks, captions, and transitions.
- Add Three.js or React Three Fiber when genuine 3D depth, lighting, and camera movement are needed.
- Create reusable components for squares, circles, triangles, grids, paths, cameras, and transitions.
- Define consistent colors, easing, motion speed, line weight, shadows, and sound cues.

## Guardrails

- Every animation must clarify an idea, reveal information, or advance the story.
- Avoid constant slow motion; vary pacing so the effect remains special.
- Do not make every scene computationally complex. Reserve full 3D transformations for major moments.
- Test whether viewers understand the subject before admiring the technique.
- Keep text readable and avoid motion that competes with narration.
