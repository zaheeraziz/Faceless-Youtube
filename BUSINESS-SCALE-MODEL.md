# YouTube Business Scale and Upside

## Official baseline

- Full ad-revenue eligibility generally requires **1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 qualified public watch hours in the previous 12 months**, or the applicable Shorts route.
- YouTube states that creators receive **55% of net Watch Page ad revenue** under the Watch Page Monetization Module.
- **RPM** is creator revenue per 1,000 views and can include ads, YouTube Premium, memberships, Super Chat, and Super Stickers. It is not advertiser-facing CPM.

Official references: [YPP eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en), [partner earnings](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72902?hl=en), [RPM definition](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314357?hl=en).

## Ads-only scenario model

There is no reliable public RPM for our exact niche. Use a range until first-party analytics replace it.

| Monthly long-form views | $2 RPM downside | $5 RPM working case | $8 RPM upside case |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100,000 | $200 | $500 | $800 |
| 500,000 | $1,000 | $2,500 | $4,000 |
| 1,000,000 | $2,000 | $5,000 | $8,000 |
| 2,000,000 | $4,000 | $10,000 | $16,000 |
| 5,000,000 | $10,000 | $25,000 | $40,000 |

Formula: **monthly revenue = monthly views ÷ 1,000 × RPM**.

These are planning scenarios, not forecasts. They are gross channel revenue before production costs, subscriptions, contractors, taxes, and overhead.

## What meaningful scale requires

At the $5 working assumption:

- $1,000/month requires about **200,000 monthly views**.
- $5,000/month requires about **1 million monthly views**.
- $10,000/month requires about **2 million monthly views**.
- $25,000/month requires about **5 million monthly views**.

Subscriber count does not produce this revenue; monetized views and audience quality do.

## Library scale illustration

| Published library | Average monthly views per video | Total monthly views | Revenue at $5 RPM |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 12 videos | 10,000 | 120,000 | $600 |
| 24 videos | 20,000 | 480,000 | $2,400 |
| 50 videos | 20,000 | 1,000,000 | $5,000 |
| 50 videos | 50,000 | 2,500,000 | $12,500 |

The difficult variable is not publishing 50 videos. It is sustaining demand across the library.

## Upside beyond ads

Potential later revenue includes sponsorships, affiliates, memberships, licensing, and digital products. Do not include these in the initial justification until the channel proves an audience and advertiser fit.

## Mentor assessment

The realistic initial objective is:

1. Reach monetization without excessive cost.
2. Demonstrate repeatable long-form views across several videos.
3. Establish first-party RPM and production-cost data.
4. Determine whether the catalog can plausibly reach 500,000–1,000,000 monthly views.

If the format cannot show repeatable discovery and retention, scaling production only increases losses.

## Questions still open

- Can newer/smaller channels in this niche repeatedly earn meaningful views?
- What is the conservative cost per finished video for our visual standard?
- How many videos can be produced monthly without quality decline?
- What percentage of catalog views persists after 90 and 180 days?
- What RPM does our actual audience geography and content earn?
- How many monthly views recover tool and production costs?
