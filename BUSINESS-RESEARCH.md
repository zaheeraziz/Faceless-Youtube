## Evidence-based ranking (August 2026)

| Rank | Concept | Verdict | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | City/country history through food | Best differentiated test | Medium |
| 2 | Historical mysteries / overlooked history | Best evidence of new-channel discovery | Medium |

| 4 | Broad world history | Huge demand, weak differentiation | Medium-high |
| 5 | Sports history | High demand, highest rights dependency | High |

### Why

- **City/country + food:** OTR (about 325K subscribers) has recent long-form videos reported around 48K–148K views, with a 518K-view Indonesia food-history episode. Strong topic evidence and a clear editorial angle. It is not faceless, so format transfer remains unproven.
- **Mystery/overlooked history:** recent self-reported small channels show early discovery (examples: 11.8K views in 28 days with a 4.3K breakout; a detailed-history channel reporting 2,690 subscribers and 8.8K views). These are anecdotes, not audited datasets, but they provide a better entry signal than food-history search produced.
- **Food history:** Tasting History (about 4.35M subscribers; 24.6M recent monthly views reported) and Weird History Food (about 774K subscribers/136M lifetime views) prove demand. Their scale and recognizable formats also raise the competitive bar.
- **Broad world history:** Timeline reports about 5.9M subscribers and 1.1B views. Demand is undeniable, but a new generic faceless history channel has little positioning advantage.
- **Sports history:** enormous audience and sponsor potential, but match footage, broadcast audio, photographs, logos, and highlight clips create recurring licensing/reused-content exposure. Original maps, statistics, narration, and licensed/public-domain visuals would be required.

### Revenue conclusion

No reliable public source exposes channel RPM. Third-party earnings estimates are too speculative for scoring. All five can support mid-roll ads in 10–20 minute videos; geography, watch time, season, and advertiser suitability will matter more than the topic label alone.

### Recommendation

Run a low-cost concept test between:

1. **Places through food:** a city's history explained through one iconic dish.
2. **Overlooked history:** a surprising true event with strong mystery/conflict but advertiser-safe treatment.

Create three titles and thumbnail concepts for each. Produce only the strongest pilot from each concept before committing to a channel identity.

## Current hypothesis

City/country history through local food is the best first candidate. It combines proven food, travel, and history interest while offering a clearer angle than broad history. Sports has high demand but presents the greatest footage-rights risk.

## Validation notes: food + place/history

- **Demand is proven:** Tasting History had about 4.35M subscribers and 24.6M views in a recent 30-day period (vidIQ, June 2026). This proves the category can support a large audience, but not that a new faceless entrant will succeed.
- **The place/culture angle performs:** OTR Food & History's recent reported videos repeatedly earned roughly 48K–148K views, including stories connecting Chinese food to Malaysian tin mines and Southeast Asian cultures.
- **Competition is real:** Tasting History, OTR, Weird History Food, and newer AI-led channels already occupy parts of the category.
- **Opportunity:** Most leading examples rely on an on-camera host, travel, or cooking. A researched faceless documentary specifically explaining cities and countries through food is more differentiated, but visual quality must substitute for host personality.
- **Warning:** AI-generated food-history channels already exist. Generic AI visuals and paraphrased scripts will not be defensible and may create inauthentic-content risk.

### Current confidence

- Audience demand: high confidence
- New-channel opportunity: medium-low confidence pending small-channel analysis
- Revenue potential: medium confidence; public RPM estimates are unreliable
- Policy/copyright safety: medium-high if visuals are owned/licensed and scripts are original

### Small/new-channel check

Public search produced several newer AI food-history channels, but not enough reliable view/subscriber data to demonstrate repeatable breakout success. This weakens the opportunity score: category demand is proven, but entry feasibility remains unproven.

**Interpretation:** Do not commit based on Tasting History or OTR. Established-channel success can hide the difficulty of acquiring an initial audience. We need a manual YouTube sample of channels created in roughly the last 24 months and their latest 10 long-form uploads.

## Evidence still required

- Identify 10 comparable channels per concept.
- Compare recent median views, upload frequency, channel size, and breakout-video rate.
- Test whether small/new channels receive meaningful views.
- Compare advertiser categories and likely RPM cautiously; creator RPM is not public.
- Audit footage sources and reused-content exposure for each format.

## Decision rule

Do not select a concept from subscriber counts or isolated viral videos. Favor concepts where multiple newer/smaller channels repeatedly earn strong views with original, repeatable formats.
